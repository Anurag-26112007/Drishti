The problem

Moving through unfamiliar indoor and outdoor spaces is hard without sight. A white cane detects what it touches, but not what is a few steps ahead or what an object is.

Existing solutions

Apps such as Be My Eyes, Microsoft Seeing AI and Google Lookout help blind users. Many need an app install, several rely on an internet connection or a volunteer, and Hindi support is often limited.

What Drishti adds

  • Opens in any phone browser, with no install.
  • Detection runs on the device, so the camera feed is not uploaded anywhere.
  • Says the object, its direction (left, ahead, right) and approximate distance.
  • Gives simple movement guidance, such as "Move slightly right".
  • Large captions plus spoken output in English and Hindi.
  • Optional custom Teachable Machine model for things the base model does not know.

How it works

Camera frames go to the COCO-SSD object detection model (TensorFlow.js). The size of each box in the frame gives a rough distance estimate, and its position gives the direction. Rules turn that into short spoken sentences using the browser's speech synthesis.

Safety: Drishti is an assistive prototype. Distances are estimates and objects can be missed. It is not a replacement for a white cane, a guide dog or a human guide.
